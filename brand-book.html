Answers you can feel.

Men's longevity, hormone health, and wellness. This book holds the shapes, the colors, the words, and the rules that keep every touchpoint recognisably ours.

Brand

The idea

Sleep, mood, energy, drive. Four separate complaints — one system.

The mark says it before a word does. Four geometric pieces, drawn on one grid, in four colors, assembling into a single S. Nothing overlaps and nothing is hidden; each part keeps its own shape while the whole becomes legible.

That is the promise and the design system at once: measure the parts, read them together, give him back one picture he can act on.

Answers you can feel.

Brand

Principles

Measure, then speak

Every claim traces to a lab value, a guideline, or a provider's judgement.

Human first, clinical second

Lead with the sentence he would say out loud. Follow with the one a clinician would sign.

Discreet by design

Privacy shows in the layout, not only the policy. No shame cues, no bodies as proof.

Mature, never macho

Confidence comes from whitespace and specificity. No flames, wolves, or "alpha."

Logo

Why this mark

SoviCare does not sell a product. It sells a composition: after the assessment and the labs, a provider assembles a plan for one man out of a shared set of components. The mark is built the same way, and that is the whole argument for it.

01
It is assembled, not drawn.

The symbol is four pieces — two halves and two quarters, each cut from a different circle. None of them belongs to the logo. All four live in the Quadrant Kit and appear on their own across packaging, decks, and the app. The mark is simply the arrangement where all four are present at once.

02
The pieces are dose forms.

A half-disc, a quarter, a capsule oval. That is the vocabulary of what actually arrives in the box: softgel, scored tablet, two-tone capsule. It is not geometry borrowed from a wellness moodboard — it is the shape language of the category the product sits in.

03
Everyone gets a different arrangement.

Two men with the same complaint rarely leave with the same plan. The kit is fixed and the composition is personal, which is exactly the promise the assessment makes. A brand built from interchangeable parts can show that; a single closed shape cannot.

Same kit, different composition

Four plans drawn from the same four pieces. A two-piece plan is not a lesser version of the mark — it is a man who needs two things.

Where the shapes come from

Each piece has a physical counterpart in the box. The resemblance is deliberate but never literal — we abstract the form, we do not illustrate the pill.

The one thing that does not vary

The mark itself is fixed: always these four pieces, at these coordinates, in this order. Modularity is a property of the kit, not a licence to redraw the logo. If every arrangement were a logo, none of them would be.

Foundations

Emphasis

A brand that promises precision cannot shout. There are four ways to emphasise, they are ranked, and only one may appear in any single paragraph.

01
Most men wait four years before asking anyone.
Weight — Inter SemiBold. The default. Two or three words maximum.
02
Symptoms are a hint. Labs are an answer.
Color — Deep Sky #1F7A94 on light, Sky on dark. One clause, never a whole sentence.
03
You don't need to know what's wrong. You need a number.
Highlight — Yellow behind Deep Teal. Once per page, for the single line that carries the argument.
04
Everything you feel
has a number behind it.
Scale change — drop the setup into Body and lift the payoff into DM Serif. The strongest device; use it on landings, not in flows.
The one-per-paragraph rule

One emphasis per paragraph, one highlight per page, and never two devices on the same sentence. Emphasis on two things in a row emphasises neither — and in a medical context it starts to read as a sales tactic.

What earns emphasis
  • A number, a range, or a timeframe.
  • Who is responsible — "a licensed provider".
  • The one thing he has to do next.
  • A reassurance that answers a real fear.
Never emphasise
  • A benefit adjective — "powerful", "proven".
  • A price or a discount.
  • Anything in the legal paragraph.
  • A symptom, ever. That is fear-selling.

The Kit

The Quadrant Kit

Six pieces, all cut from the same square. Every graphic in the brand — backgrounds, icons, image overlays, dividers, data — is assembled from these and nothing else. That is what makes an unfamiliar layout still look like us.

The Kit

Composition rules

01
Snap to the grid

Every piece sits on the 8pt grid at 1, 2, 3 or 4 units. No arbitrary sizes.

02
Three colors, one accent

Max three colors per composition. Yellow may appear once, and always as the smallest piece.

03
Bleed or touch

A piece either runs off the edge or sits fully inside. Never crop it 90% and leave a sliver.

04
Curves face the content

The convex side points toward the headline, so the shape frames rather than shoves.

The Kit

Iconography

Solid, not outline — the icons are the kit at small scale. A Mist body carries the form; one or two brand colors mark the part that carries the meaning, and yellow appears at most once. 48px grid, quarter-unit minimum.

The Kit

Numerals

Step numbers, chapter marks, and countdowns are built from the kit rather than set in type. They only ever run 1 to 4 — the four steps of the journey — so the set stays small and deliberate.

Imagery

Image treatment

A photograph never appears bare. At least one piece from the kit lands on it — sometimes solid and cropping the frame, sometimes translucent and letting the image read through. Drop your own images onto any frame below.

Imagery

Capsule module

A second image device, for grids rather than single frames. The stadium — a square stretched with fully round ends — tiles vertically and horizontally on the same 8pt grid. Some capsules hold flat color, some hold a photograph, and the rhythm between them is the composition.

Ratio — 1×1, 1×2, 2×1 or 1×3 only
Photos — no more than a third of the tiles
Radius — always the full short edge
Imagery

Photo as a piece

The stronger move. Instead of laying a shape over a photograph, cut the photograph into a shape from the kit — the image becomes one of the quadrants rather than a backdrop with decoration on it. Use this for posters, covers, and anything that has to carry the brand on its own.

Cutting the photo
  • Only kit shapes: circle, half, quarter, arch. Never an oval or a soft-cornered rectangle.
  • One cut image per layout, two at most — a page of circles becomes a mood board.
  • Crop inside the shape, not to it. The subject should look framed, not trimmed.
  • A cut photo counts as one of your three colors.
Warm ground

Poster layouts may sit on Clay #CFC6B8 instead of Mist when the photography is warm. It is the one neutral outside the core palette, and it never appears in product UI.

Imagery

Overlay rules

Always
  • One to four pieces. Four is a busy maximum.
  • At least one piece anchored to an edge or corner.
  • Solid pieces sit at the edges; translucent pieces sit over the subject.
  • Translucency is 80–90%, never below 55% — it should read as color, not as a filter.
  • Type sits on a solid piece or on clear sky. Never half-on.
Never
  • A piece covering a face, or landing on a body part as a joke.
  • Rotated pieces — the kit is axis-aligned. Only the arc may rotate, in 90° steps.
  • Gradients, blurs, or drop shadows on a piece.
  • Duotone or heavily graded photography. Color stays true.
  • The full logo used as an overlay shape.
Piece sizing
Anchor piece — 25–35% of the frame's short edge
Secondary — 15–20%
Accent — under 10%, always yellow
Imagery

Casting & subjects

Three subject families, mixed roughly 2:1:1. Men in ordinary moments do the heavy lifting; landscape and detail give the layout air.

Direction notes
  • Natural light, true color, no teal-and-orange grade.
  • Real ages and real bodies. No gym physiques.
  • No clinics, scrubs, stethoscopes, or pill close-ups.
  • No couples looking wistfully into the middle distance.
  • Shoot wide with corner room — the kit needs somewhere to land.
  • Portraits shown here are reference comps, not licensed assets. Replace before anything ships.

Strategy

Insights

Five observations the brand is built on. Each one has a design consequence — if a decision does not trace back to one of these, it is decoration.

01
He is not avoiding the doctor. He is avoiding the conversation.

The barrier is rarely time or money. It is having to say the words out loud to a stranger, in a room, with no idea whether what he is describing is even a real thing.

So: Lead every entry point with a form, not a phone number. The first exchange should be silent.
02
Symptoms are shared. Causes are not.

Fatigue, low mood, weight, low libido and poor sleep all present identically across half a dozen unrelated causes. He has usually already self-diagnosed, and usually wrongly.

So: Never organise the product by symptom alone. Every path routes through measurement before it names anything.
03
He trusts numbers more than he trusts reassurance.

Told "you're fine", he assumes he was dismissed. Shown 18.2 nmol/L against a range, he believes it — even when the answer is the same.

So: Show the value, the range, and the date on every result. Never summarise a result without displaying it.
04
The category has taught him to expect a hard sell.

The competitive set is dominated by subscription testosterone brands with gym imagery and outcome promises. He arrives braced for it, and reads warmth as a tactic.

So: Underclaim. Say what will not help him. A brand that says no is the only one he will believe when it says yes.
05
The moment of intent is short and usually private.

Searches happen late at night, on a phone, alone. If the first screen asks him to create an account, he leaves and does not come back.

So: No account before value. The assessment starts on tap one and asks for an email at the end, not the beginning.

Strategy

Message house

One idea at the top, three pillars underneath, and the proof each pillar is allowed to lean on. Everything written for the brand should be traceable to one box on this page.

The one idea
Answers you can feel.

Men's health has been sold as ambition — be stronger, be sharper, be twenty-five again. SoviCare sells the step before that: finding out what is actually going on, from people qualified to say, and doing something about the part that is real.

Pillar 01
Start with a measurement

Nothing is named, treated, or promised before it is measured. The assessment and the panel come first, always, even when it costs us the faster conversion.

Proof — at-home panel, accredited lab, values with reference ranges and collection date on every result.
Pillar 02
A person signs the answer

A licensed provider reads the whole chart and puts their name to what happens next. Not an algorithm, not a quiz score, not a "wellness score".

Proof — named clinicians, fifteen-minute review call, written rationale for every recommendation and every decline.
Pillar 03
Discreet, and easy to stop

Plain packaging, no shame cues, and a cancel that takes one tap. Confidence in the product means never needing to trap anyone in it.

Proof — unbranded outer packaging, one-tap cancellation, results exportable and portable at any time.
Positioning statement

For men over forty who have felt off for a while and done nothing about it, SoviCare is a men's health service that starts with a measurement instead of a promise — because the only thing worth acting on is the part you can actually see.

Claims we do not make
  • Any outcome with a timeframe attached.
  • Comparisons to a named competitor.
  • Before-and-after imagery of any kind.
  • "Clinically proven" without a citation on the same screen.
  • Anything about performance, prowess, or partners.
Voice

Voice & tone

Two registers, always in this order: the human sentence, then the precise one. Never one without the other.

Human — DM Serif

You've been putting this off. That's normal.

  • Second person, present tense.
  • Short sentences with full stops, not fragments.
  • Says the awkward thing plainly and moves on.
Precise — Inter

A licensed provider reviews your assessment and lab results before any treatment is offered.

  • Names who does what, and when.
  • No hedging adverbs — "may" and "can" only where clinically true.
  • Numbers carry units and a source on file.
Say
  • "Start with answers"
  • "Your provider will explain what this means"
  • "Below the reference range"
  • "Fifteen minutes, by phone"
  • "Nothing here needs treating"
Never say
  • "Optimize your testosterone"
  • "Unlock your potential" / "peak performance"
  • "Low T" as a diagnosis
  • "Reverse aging" / "biohack"
  • Anything with "alpha," "beast," or "prime"
Applications

Presentation

Six layouts, 1920 × 1080, 80px margin. A deck alternates between Deep Teal and Mist grounds — never two dark slides in a row, never more than three of any one layout. Body copy never goes below 24px.

Deck rules
  • 1920 × 1080, 80px margin, 12-column grid.
  • Title 88px, statement 64px, body 28px, caption 24px. Nothing smaller.
  • One idea per slide. If it needs two columns of body copy, it is two slides.
  • Alternate grounds — never two Deep Teal slides consecutively.
  • One kit piece per slide, bleeding off at least one edge.
Slide furniture
  • Logo on the title and closing slide only.
  • No slide numbers, no footer, no repeated logo bug.
  • Section dividers use the numerals from the kit, 1 to 4.
  • Sources go on the slide they support, at 24px, never in an appendix.

Motion

The kit is made of arcs, so the brand moves along arcs. Nothing fades in from nowhere and nothing bounces — a piece either sweeps along its own radius or pivots around the corner it is anchored to. If a movement could not be drawn with a compass, it is not ours.

Loader
Three quadrants at descending opacity, 1.4s linear, continuous. The only element in the system permitted to loop.
Tokens
Instant100msHover, focus ring, checkbox
Quick180msButtons, chips, toggles
Base280msType rise, card enter, tooltips
Considered420msShape wipe, panel slide, tab change
Deliberate520msPivot, page transition, result reveal
Easing
Enter cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1) — things arriving — decelerate hard
Exit cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 1, 1) — things leaving — accelerate away
Move cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1) — things already on screen
Loop linear — the loader, and nothing else
Rules
  • One animated element per view. A page where three things move is a page nobody reads.
  • Shapes wipe or pivot. Type rises. Numbers count. Nothing else has a motion of its own.
  • Movement is always toward the content, never away from it — a piece arrives to frame, not to distract.
  • Stagger is 90ms, three items maximum. Beyond that it reads as a loading failure.
  • Everything plays once. Only the loader loops.
In product
  • Assessment steps — the outgoing question wipes left, the next rises. 280ms, no crossfade.
  • Progress — quadrants fill one at a time, 420ms each. The shape is the progress bar.
  • Result reveal — range bar wipes in, then the value counts, then the marker settles. Strictly in that order.
  • Errors — no shake, ever. The field border changes color in 100ms and the message rises.
Never
  • Bounce, elastic, or overshoot easing. It reads as playful, and this is medical.
  • Parallax, scroll-jacking, or anything that moves while he is reading.
  • Blur, glow, or morphing between two kit pieces.
  • Animating a legal disclaimer into view. It is present on load or it is not compliant.
  • Autoplaying video with a face in it above the fold.
Reduced motion

Under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce every animation and transition in the system is disabled outright — not shortened, not softened. Nothing in the brand depends on movement to be understood, so removing all of it costs nothing. This page already honours it.

Sachets and pouches are where the range gets carried, opened in an office kitchen, and left on a hotel desk. They are the most public thing SoviCare makes, so they are also the most discreet: the product name is small, the day is large, and nothing on the outside says what it treats.

Discretion first
  • Nothing on the outside states what the product treats.
  • No symptom words, no anatomy, no "men's" anything.
  • The dose instruction is the largest text after the day number.
  • Outer mailers are unbranded. The sachet inside carries the brand.
Format rules
  • Sachet corners are 3px — nearly square. Only bottles get the 16px radius.
  • One kit shape per pouch, none at all on a daily sachet.
