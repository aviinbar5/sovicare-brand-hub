06 Male menopause / andropause education Two variants
UX purpose Give the experience a name and a mechanism, so it stops being a personal failing and becomes a thing that can be looked at.
User need "Is this just age, and is it supposed to feel like this?" Most men have never been told there is a name for it.
Conversion role Legitimises the category. The visitor who reads this converts later but converts more confidently.
Hierarchy · CTAs Editorial headline → plain-language explanation → what it is not. Primary: Start with answers. Secondary: Read the guide.
A Editorial spread · portrait and plain language Recommended Reads like a serious magazine rather than a clinic leaflet, which is what makes a man finish it.
Andropause It has a name, and it is not a character flaw. From around forty, testosterone declines slowly — roughly one per cent a year for most men. Slowly enough that nothing announces itself, and fast enough that a decade later the man in the mirror sleeps worse, recovers slower and cares less. It is not a diagnosis on its own, and it is not the explanation for everything. Several unrelated things produce the same feeling, which is exactly why we measure before we name anything. Start with answers Read the guide
B Myth and fact pairs Corrects misconceptions efficiently, but repeating a myth in large type is still repeating it.
Often said "It is just getting older." Closer to it Age explains the decline. It does not explain how you feel about it, and it does not mean nothing can be measured or changed.
Often said "Low energy means low testosterone." Closer to it Thyroid, sleep, iron, mood and several other things produce the same week. Symptoms are a hint. Labs are an answer.
Often said "Treatment means being on something forever." Closer to it Sometimes the answer is sleep, weight or a deficiency. What may be appropriate is a clinician's decision, based on eligibility — and it is reviewed as you go.
