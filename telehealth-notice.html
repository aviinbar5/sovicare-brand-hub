Telehealth Notice

How care works when it happens online, what a provider can and cannot decide from a distance, and what you are agreeing to.

For emergenciesSoviCare is not an emergency medical service. Do not use this website or wait for a provider response if you are experiencing a medical or mental health emergency. Call 911 or go to the nearest emergency department.

1About telehealth care

Telehealth allows you to share health information and receive care using electronic communications. Depending on your needs, this may include an online assessment, messages, a phone or video consultation, and follow-up care.

Medical care available through SoviCare is provided by licensed healthcare professionals. SoviCare provides technology and administrative support for the care process. A provider makes all clinical decisions, including whether a treatment or prescription is appropriate for you.

2What to expect

Your provider may rely on the information you submit during your assessment, along with any additional information they request. Please answer health questions accurately and tell your provider about relevant medical conditions, medications, allergies, and changes in your health.

Telehealth has limitations. A provider may determine that they need more information, testing, or an in-person examination before recommending treatment. They may also decide that treatment through SoviCare is not appropriate for you. Payment for a consultation does not guarantee a prescription or the dispensing of medication.

Electronic communications can occasionally be delayed or interrupted. Check your account and the contact methods you provide for messages about your care. Do not use routine messages for urgent medical concerns.

3Your information

Your health information may be shared with the licensed professionals and service partners involved in your care, as described in the applicable Privacy Policy and Notice of Privacy Practices. Please review those notices to understand how your information is used and protected.

4Your choice to use telehealth

You may ask your provider questions about the proposed care, including its potential benefits, risks, and alternatives. You may decline telehealth care and seek in-person care elsewhere. If you choose to withdraw consent after care has begun, tell your provider so they can explain how that decision affects your treatment.

5Compounded medications

If a provider determines that a compounded medication is appropriate for your individual medical needs, it may be prepared and dispensed by a licensed pharmacy. Compounded medications are not FDA-approved, and the FDA does not review them for safety, effectiveness, or quality before they are marketed.

6Consent

By selecting "I agree" when asked for telehealth consent, I acknowledge that I have read this notice, understand the nature and limitations of telehealth, and consent to receive care through telehealth from a licensed healthcare provider.
